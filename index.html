Hi, I'm Shreyam

 
 

I study how intelligent agents build internal models of the world, and how they learn which of their own predictions deserve to be acted on.

Autonomous Systems Researcher @ Maruti Suzuki R&D
B.Tech, Mechanical Engineering @ IIT (BHU) Varanasi

I believe embodied AI will not be built on better pixels, but on world models that can tell when their own predictions are surprising.

Mechanical Engineering taught me to think in state, dynamics, and cause, and I've carried that into world models: occupancy models for planning at Team AVERERA , a critic trained on human interventions, and a semantic world model on frozen DINOv2 features under Prof. Shishir Kolathaya at Stochastic Robotics Lab, IISc.

That critic could only recognize what humans had already flagged. My question: how can a system develop its own feedback signals, such as novelty or surprise, without an external verifier? My bet is they should come from learned semantic representations, not raw prediction error. An early hint came at NIC (National Informatics Centre) : giving each moving region in traffic CCTV its own latent code over frozen DINOv2 features made sudden maneuvers stand out with no labeled accident data.
